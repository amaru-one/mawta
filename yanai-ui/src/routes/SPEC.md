## Purpose
SvelteKit routes — the app's screens. All pages are client-rendered (static SPA).

## Contents
- `+layout.ts` — disables SSR/prerender as needed for SPA mode; `+layout.svelte` — global shell (loads `app.css`, icon sprite, toast) and the **auth gate**: on `/login` it sets `session` to `anonymous` and fires no probe (that route must be reachable with no session at all); everywhere else it calls `GET /me` on mount, and only on success does it populate the teacher identity, the school's `usesInternalGrading` switch, `session`, and start the notification poll (previously unconditional — running it while signed out spammed `/notifications` with 401s, each one re-triggering the redirect below). Registers `setUnauthorizedHandler` once, which any 401 elsewhere in the app also routes through — clearing `session`/`teacher` and navigating to `/login`. `{@render children()}` is withheld while `session.status === 'unknown'`, so there is never a flash of app content before that redirect. Still tracks `canGoBack` via `afterNavigate` for `TopBar`'s `useHistory` arrow.
- `login/` — the only unguarded route: username + password against `POST /auth/login` (see its own SPEC.md).
- `+page.svelte` — **Home: one day, on an hour grid.** A fixed header — the date (`Jueves 3 SET`), a mono line counting the day and its span, and a **Monday-to-Friday strip** — over a **proportional time grid** that is the only thing that scrolls. Every session on the grid opens that session's recording screen (`/class/{courseId}/lesson/{sessionId}`), never the área: **a tap on a lesson is a tap on that lesson**, and the voice note it leads to is bound to the session you touched. That holds for future days too, deliberately — the rule is "the row you touched is the session you record into", and carving out an exception for tomorrow would make the destination something the teacher has to predict. The área is reached from the recording screen's "Ir a {área}" button — a bubble opened from Home always carries it, since Home never links to área directly (see that screen's SPEC for when the button is withheld instead). **There is no search on this screen.** It filtered the grid by class name; it was removed for being redundant with the strip, which already picks the day, and nothing replaces it.
  - **One hour is always 64px.** That single number is the whole mechanism: a 90-minute class draws twice a 45-minute one, and a break is exactly the space it occupies. It is why **gaps are not rendered and not labelled** — the emptiness between two boxes *is* the recreo, so the app never has to decide whether a hole is a break or an unassigned hour, which it cannot know.
  - **The window is fixed at 07:00–17:00**, the same every day, and stretches only for a day with something outside it. A grid that shrank to fit each day would move the scale under the teacher's feet on every tap and make Tuesday incomparable to Wednesday; a short day is meant to read as mostly empty.
  - **A box is filled *and* outlined**, and both are load-bearing. The fill (`--surface-1`) separates busy from free; the 1px `--border-strong` outline separates two classes that touch — without it the 2px seam between a 10:00–11:30 and an 11:30–12:15 reads as a scratch inside one long box rather than as the end of one and the start of another.
  - **Each box states its own start and end** (`08:00 – 09:30 · Aula 12`, mono, above the name). The gutter only marks whole hours, so without this a class starting at 11:30 or 13:15 would have its time written nowhere on the screen. The geometry says how *long*; only the text says *when*.
  - Hour lines are **1px**, not the app's 0.5px hairline: at half a pixel the scale fades out and the grid stops reading as time. The now marker is a **solid** black rule, not the old rail's 35% — it crosses filled boxes.
  - **The strip is the whole of the day navigation, and the week on screen is the only week there is.** Five cells, Monday to Friday; no arrows and no paging into the previous or next week. There were yesterday/tomorrow arrows for exactly as long as they could leave the week — once navigation was bounded they did nothing the strip did not already do, and a second control for the same five destinations is a control to delete, not to keep. The dot under a number says that day has classes; a thin ring marks where today sits while another day is being looked at.
  - **The screen loads once and never fetches again.** `GET /home` answers with the asked-for day in `today` and the *rest* of its ISO week in `week` — `repo.GetHome` excludes the day itself (`session_date <> $2`) — so the union covers exactly the week the strip can reach. `getHome`'s `date` parameter is left unused on purpose; nothing here can ask for another week. `week` carries only days that have sessions, so the strip is built from computed dates, not from the payload.
  - **A weekend "today" lands on that week's Friday.** The strip has no Saturday or Sunday cell, so opening the app on one would otherwise leave no day selected and both arrows dead. Friday is the last school day and is inside the ISO week the payload already covers, so it needs no second request.
  - **A day with no classes shows no grid at all** — one line, not ten empty hours taking the whole screen to say the same thing.
  - Only the grid scrolls; the header and strip are pinned. Ten hours at 64px do not fit under the strip on a phone, and losing the day picker in order to see the afternoon would be the worse trade. The grid **opens scrolled to the current hour** (or to the first class on another day), untracked so a clock tick never yanks the teacher's scroll back.
  - Session state is **clock-derived**, as on the área: `en curso` while the wall clock sits inside a session, muted once it has ended. `status` is consulted only to mark a `cancelled`/`rescheduled` session, which renders muted, labelled `cancelada`, and is not a link. `now` refreshes on a 60s interval.
  - **Nothing on this screen is a to-do.** No pending-grade counts, no missing-attendance badges, no unwritten-comment prompts — the agenda states facts about time. The teacher's work queue is the notifications feed, reached from the bell.
  - The class happening **right now** is the one loud element: it takes the action gradient and white type, while the rest of the day sits on `--aurora-tint` and a finished class steps back to the page's own white. (This replaces the old rule that emphasis was black and never the accent — see the Conventions entry below.)
- `class/[courseId]/` — **the área**: the class seen whole — its past, current and upcoming sessions, plus its criterios and class-wide evidencia. A reference screen and a launcher, not a destination in itself (see its SPEC.md).
- `class/[courseId]/lesson/[sessionId]/` — the lesson recording screen, and **the app's primary destination**: find a student by name, hold to leave a voice note (see its SPEC.md).
- `class/[courseId]/students/` — **Alumnos**: the class as a list of people, scoped to one competencia. It reports and navigates; it does not evaluate (see its SPEC.md).
- `class/[courseId]/students/[studentId]/` — **the student's screen, where evaluating happens**: the period's evidence with its values, then the nivel de logro of the competencia (see its SPEC.md).
- `class/[courseId]/criteria/` — where the teacher writes the criterios de evaluación for each competencia (see its SPEC.md).
- `class/[courseId]/evidence/new/` — registering one evaluation for the class or for one student, tagged with the competencia and criterios it speaks to (see its SPEC.md).
- `grades/[studentId]/` — student grades (see its SPEC.md).
- `notifications/` — notification feed (see its SPEC.md).
- `profile/` — teacher profile (see its SPEC.md).

## Inputs / Outputs
Pages call `lib/` orchestration modules and endpoints, render `lib/components`.

## Conventions & Constraints
- Each route is documented by its own SPEC.md, which is the product spec for that screen — there is no separate mock or gallery to keep in step with. A new screen gets a SPEC.md in the same turn it's added.
- **Every screen wears the same four things**, all defined in `app.css`: a 34px/700 title with a 1.5px rule under it, a mono uppercase context line beneath that, mono `.eyebrow` section labels, and one primary action per screen on the action gradient (secondary ones on `--aurora-tint`). A screen that needs different numbers overrides them locally and says why in its own SPEC.
- **The assistant is marked by SHAPE, not by colour.** `#5b5fc7` used to be the app's one signal for assistant-generated content, and that worked while everything else was grey. Now that the ink itself is indigo, the accent alone no longer distinguishes anything: an assistant row carries a **filled** badge with its glyph, while what the app derives itself carries an **outlined** one on the page's white. The accent still fills that badge and still marks the AD tier and the bell's unread dot — but the fill, not the hue, is what carries the meaning.
